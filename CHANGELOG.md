# Changelog

All notable changes to `Boutquin.Numerics` are documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). Versions are produced by [MinVer](https://github.com/adamralph/minver) from git tags on the public release repository.

## [2.0.0] — Unreleased

Final-version candidate; publication is pending. This major version updates the generic-math API, numerical contracts and verification coverage.

### Changed

- Correct numerical documentation for normal-CDF precision, QIS eigendecomposition cost, finite-threshold tail summaries, spline convergence/continuity/extrapolation, and clipped single-factor shrinkage. Clarify OLS precision choices, the legacy Jacobi precision conversion, and which convenience facades carry deprecation warnings.

- Consolidate Levenberg–Marquardt callback paths into one iteration engine and monotone-cubic implementations into one tangent routine. Remove redundant covariance normalization work and unused helpers.
- Remove binary-only constructor overloads from generic NERCOME, POET and doubly-sparse estimators. Existing nullable constructor entry points remain. Historical facade parity, iteration counts and output-bit snapshots no longer constrain the 2.0 implementation; independent mathematical accuracy, numeric-type, range, deterministic-operation and allocation checks remain.

- Add automatic Newey–West bandwidth selection with VAR(1) prewhitening, residual Bartlett HAC and recolouring through `LongRunCovariance` and `MeanVarianceAutomatic`. Keep `AutomaticLags(int)` as a pilot-window helper and retain explicit-lag estimates; reject counts that cannot form finite positive divisors in the selected numeric type.
- Add full-panel Holm and BHY adjustments through `HaircutSharpe.ComputePanel` and `AdjustPValues`, and trial-Sharpe mean and variance inputs through `DeflatedSharpeRatio.ComputeWithTrialMoments`. Existing scalar haircut and zero-mean DSR overloads retain their documented compatibility assumptions.
- Use constant-forward extrapolation outside the Hagan–West interpolation range.
- Make graphical lasso penalize all entries, including the diagonal, by default; stop on mean absolute covariance change scaled by the mean absolute off-diagonal sample covariance, with default tolerance 0.001.
- Implement NERCOME using zero-mean second moments divided by the observation count, replicated full-matrix averaging, and the published split-grid selector. OAS likewise uses the known-zero-mean second moment divided by the observation count.
- Seed GARCH from residual second moments divided by the observation count, constrain omega to the variance target at every parameter trial, and report average per-observation log likelihood. Provide a fixed-zero-mean option and constrain the optional AR(1) mean extension to stationarity.
- Reject nonfinite generated Nelder–Mead coordinates before evaluating the objective, and apply the Gao–Han iteration-counter termination convention.

- Root stopping now follows the selected Numerical Recipes listings: bracketed Newton accepts endpoint-equality candidates and stops on a strictly small computed step or stagnation; Secant's strict computed-step stop no longer requires a residual sign change. Bisection uses strict halved-step stopping and returns the nonpositive-function endpoint on that stop. Residual/endpoint and result-based exhaustion extensions remain. A convergence flag can therefore accompany a large residual; the unbracketed Newton residual safeguard is unchanged.

- Breaking tied-data correction: `FastDoubleBootstrap` now excludes equality at both empirical ranks. Right tails count strictly greater statistics, and left tails strictly smaller statistics, following Davidson (2023). Previously both included ties. Two-sided values fold the corrected one-sided values; Type-7 inner quantiles are unchanged.

- Lower-tail result documentation distinguishes the finite-threshold sample estimate from the population limit; a zero sample estimate does not establish asymptotic independence.
- Graphical-lasso convergence now requires all inner coordinate cycles to converge, preventing exhausted inner iterations from reporting success merely because the repeated precision estimate stops changing.

- Breaking v2 RC correction: `GeneralizationScore<T>` / `GtScoreResult<T>` and their concrete facades are replaced by `GtInspiredScore<T>` / `GtInspiredScoreResult<T>`. The variant uses sample SD, cumulative arithmetic-return R², zero-target downside RMS over all observations, and additive epsilon. Existing numerical behavior is retained; no author parity or empirical generalization claim is made.
- `GtInspiredScore<T>` applies the authenticated author's inclusive minimum-count penalty through 50 observations. A new overload lets callers choose the threshold for their sampling frequency; component and smoothing definitions are unchanged.

- `QSpline` and `RevisedNotAKnot` now follow Jarre’s JCAM 478 (2026), Appendix, authenticated by author code dated October 29, 2025. Q adds fifth-difference damping and now requires six knots; RNAK uses the journal’s factor-5/2 local safeguard instead of the July preprint’s two-stage rule. Existing July reference fixtures remain historical; thirty native author cases independently verify the successor rules.

- `EigenClip` now accepts an `eigenvalueFloor` and projects each eigenvalue to `max(λ, δ)`. The default remains zero; a positive floor now raises smaller eigenvalues instead of discarding them. The parameter rename and changed positive-floor behavior are intentional 2.0 RC breaking changes.

- Upgrade PublicApiAnalyzers to 5.6.0 and declare 258 existing compiler-generated record and delegate members in the shipped API baseline. Runtime APIs and implementations are unchanged; the new analyzer now checks these members explicitly.

- `GarchOneOneEstimator<T>`: the estimated persistence sum `α̂+β̂` is now strictly enforced below 1 by
  construction (previously it could round to exactly `1.0` in IEEE arithmetic on near-integrated / IGARCH-like
  data, contradicting the documented stationarity invariant and causing `ForecastVariance(horizon > 1)` to
  throw on a freshly-fitted result). Estimates saturate a tiny margin below the ceiling for integrated /
  near-integrated data; non-integrated fits are numerically unaffected.
- `WildBootstrapResampler<T>` and `WildBootstrapResampler` constructors now reject undefined `WildBootstrapWeights` enum values with `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` at construction, naming the supported families (Mammen, Rademacher, Gaussian). Previously an out-of-range cast (`(WildBootstrapWeights)42`) was accepted by the constructor and surfaced later as a generic `InvalidOperationException("Unknown weight family: …")` from `Resample` once `NextWeight` was invoked. Callers passing only the three documented enum values are unaffected.
- `ExponentiallyWeightedCovarianceEstimator<T>` now generates and normalises geometric weights entirely in `T`; `PoetCovarianceEstimator<T>` computes its threshold in `T`; and the QIS bandwidth, kernel, density, Hilbert-transform, and eigenvalue-shrinkage path now remains in `T` (Tier A + √) rather than being bounded by an internal `double` conversion.

### Fixed

- Regenerate every computed verification-vector family with the required Graphical Lasso metadata. Publish standalone vectors and QP corpus files atomically, and fully refresh reused census mutation build inputs.
- Expose StressRunner's existing test-tool dependency roots to dependency updaters so its lock file participates in test-package updates without enabling test SDK build hooks.

- Reduce `ActiveSetQpSolver<T>` long-short solve and refusal costs by reusing unchanged Cholesky columns, fraction-free factors and exact right-hand-side updates across working sets. An exact norm witness can establish the conditioning refusal for `double` without forming the full adjugate; numerical acceptance thresholds and refusal messages are unchanged.

- Reduce the cost of `ActiveSetQpSolver<T>` solves that change their working set many times, long-short boxes in particular. The exact gradient `Σw`, evaluated several times per working-set change, now forms each row in one integer accumulator instead of one arbitrary-precision product and one sum per term. A long-short `double` mean-variance solve of order 150 takes about half the time and allocates about a third of the memory it did. The gradient is the same exact value, so every returned weight, refusal and exception message is unchanged.

- `ActiveSetQpSolver<T>.SolveMeanVariance` with `riskAversion = 0` now uses one exact greedy allocation for every feasible finite-budget box. It forms the budget and capacities in exact arithmetic, fills in descending mean order (index order for ties), and rounds only the partially filled weight. This removes the rounded-fill threshold, normalization and residual redistribution. Ordinary outputs can change in their last bits; mathematical accuracy, bound adherence and deterministic ties replace historical output compatibility.

- `ActiveSetQpSolver<T>.SolveMinVariance` and `SolveMeanVariance` (`λ > 0`) now run a primal-feasible active set with a budget null-space step, an exact rational route where rounded arithmetic cannot decide, and an independent KKT certificate on the returned vector; the previous bound-fixing core, its verification patch and its release normalisation are removed. Fixes: a budget-violating result when the budget-orthogonal part of `Σ⁻¹μ` fell below the rounding of the budget solve; refusals of solvable problems whose inverse-solve components overflowed although the optimum is representable (decimal in particular); active sets that cycled, so that valid problems were refused as "did not converge"; a release test that normalised by the largest gradient of every asset and so masked a free asset's sign violation; and a verification step that accepted a grossly wrong decimal vector yet refused verifiable ones. A result is never returned unchecked, and a refusal now names the failed certificate component (budget, bound, stationarity or multiplier sign). Problems at the equal-weight frontier (`n · maxWeight = 1`, for example `n = 7` or `n = 10`) are solved instead of being refused as "did not converge", and the minimum-variance weights are invariant to a positive rescaling of the covariance (at `double`, bit-identical for a factor that is a power of two and within 2.2e-16 for the other factors tried), because the problem is equilibrated once and every certificate component is measured against its own row's scale. For `SolveMeanVariance` the optimum of `max w′μ − (λ/2)·w′Σw` moves when only the covariance is rescaled; it is unchanged when the covariance is multiplied by a factor and `riskAversion` is divided by it (bit-identical for the factor 4 in the case tried). Behaviour changes where the previous result was incorrect or was a refusal of a solvable problem, and in one further class that is a new refusal: a problem whose block of the covariance that decides the result is numerically singular at the working precision (`48·n·u·κ₁ ≥ 1` for the exact 1-norm condition number `κ₁` of the equilibrated block, `u` the unit roundoff; for example near-duplicate assets: a two-asset block with correlation within roughly `2e-14` of 1) is now refused with `InvalidOperationException` naming stationarity, even where the previous core returned a vector accurate to working precision, because no residual can verify such a vector. For `decimal` the equilibration is limited to exact radix-10 shifts that keep every scaled entry within 28 decimal places, so the measured block is not always the unit-scale one: a covariance with 27 or 28 significant digits can be refused inside the stated margin of its unit-scale form, by up to the ratio of its diagonal scales (two assets with unit variances and `1 − ρ = 4.5e-26` have `48·n·u·κ₁ = 0.43` for the unit-scale form and are refused, while the same correlation with variances 1 and 100 was answered in all 381 cases tried inside the margin). A problem whose deciding block stays below that margin is answered as before; assets held at their bounds do not enter the measured block unless the solver could not act on a materially negative multiplier of theirs. The rounded route allows `8n + 8` working-set changes and then hands the problem to an exact rational route that allows `12n + 12`; exhausting the exact route's budget is a refusal. Internal pre-checks in `double` (Cholesky-based proofs, in `double` and double-double arithmetic, of positive definiteness or of the order of the first non-positive leading minor, and certified bounds on the conditioning of each working set's block and of the deciding block: a condition-number bound from an approximate inverse, or a proved lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of the whole covariance or of the block) decide only what an exact test would decide, so an ill-conditioned `decimal` covariance inside the conditioning margin, including a near-singular one, and a covariance refused for positive definiteness with a negative leading minor, a repeated asset or a dependence on a short coefficient grid are decided in O(n³) operations rather than by exact elimination. The exact tests remain for a block near or past the conditioning margin (`48·n·u·κ₁` above about `1/(2√m)` for a block of order `m`) and for a covariance whose smallest leading eigenvalue is below the double-double resolution (at most `32·(n + 3)·n·2⁻¹⁰⁶` of the largest diagonal entry) or whose first non-positive leading minor is exactly zero without such a dependence; every returned weight is computed in `T`. Elsewhere results may differ in the last bits. The public API and the exception types are unchanged, and so is the `riskAversion = 0` linear-programme result, except for the vertices that the separate `riskAversion = 0` entry above recomputes in exact arithmetic, namely those with `(n + 2) · |1 − n · minWeight| > 1024`, which include large-budget short-bound vertices, ordinary long-short boxes whose formula value exceeds 1024 (for example `n = 100` with `minWeight = −0.1`), every box with `minWeight = 0` and 1023 or more assets, and positive-floor boxes whose formula value exceeds 1024.
- Preserve centered fourth moments and market-factor moments under representable large translations in the Ledoit–Wolf estimators and POET residual thresholds.
- Preserve representable subnormal sample covariances and DSR standard errors; GARCH forecasts skip zero-weight innovations and retain finite weighted innovations when the raw residual overflows.
- Translate decimal VAR or recolouring inversion overflow into the documented `InvalidOperationException` with a numerical noninvertibility explanation.

- Half graphical lasso recognizes exact stationary coordinate solutions while retaining the exhausted-inner-cycle safeguard. Long Half BHY panels accumulate the harmonic correction and adjusted probabilities in double before rounding each output.
- GT-inspired loss retains a finite logarithm when its significance diagnostic overflows, preserving the selected library variant. Haircut Sharpe retains representable Student-t tails when squaring a large finite statistic overflows.
- EWMA covariance scales large mixed-sign columns before anchor subtraction, preserving finite weighted covariances across extreme endpoints while keeping small subnormal variances and decimal translation accuracy.

- QR response scaling accounts for reflector magnitude, preserving finite float least-squares fits. Decimal cubic splines retain tiny distinct intervals during spacing normalization, and standardized decimal PCA preserves representable centered values during fitting and projection. Jacobi reports decimal rotation overflow with the documented `InvalidOperationException`.

- Keep Half sample excess kurtosis finite at the accepted 65,519-observation boundary when the corrected moment is representable.
- Compute Kendall tau-b for two observations from their single pair, including concordant, discordant and tied inputs; Spearman retains its fewer-than-three convention.
- Adapt sub-one Politis–White estimates to the stationary resampler's minimum mean block length in the bootstrap example, and display both values.

- `GarchOneOneEstimator<T>.Fit` normalizes eligible tiny and large return scales before numerical underflow or overflow invalidates its initial simplex. It explicitly rejects unrepresentable initial or restored positive intercepts and variances, and prevents repeated normalization at unchanged unit scale.

- Half streaming moments retain rounded-off mean increments and sample skewness retains small cubed contributions; decimal P² tail estimates check rounded marker bounds before rescaling extreme finite values.
- Moment documentation states the Half count ceiling, Harrell–Davis variance guidance is distribution-dependent, and PCG stream documentation describes the full 65-bit derived increment.

- LM requires projected-gradient stationarity when clipping a step to bounds; tiny accepted or rejected projections no longer certify convergence by step or cost tolerance alone. Secant starts its current iterate at the initial point with the smaller residual.
- Stress runs report a host anomaly when their summary cannot be persisted, while preserving numerical-failure precedence.
- Corrected solver tolerance, covariance, damping, precision, bound-relaxation, tier, benchmark, and example documentation; strengthened IEEE square-root bit and stress-input hash checks.

- Preserved finite results when denoising covariance traces, finite-horizon GARCH forecasts, and GT-inspired benchmark differences exceed the working type's intermediate range.
- Preserved supported Half results for high-frequency Sharpe annualization, long-series GARCH initial variance, and NERCOME rotated variances; the POET double facade now rejects the rounded decimal boundary with its documented parameter exception.

- Mixed-width cubic interpolation avoids reciprocal-first overflow; unrepresentable spline coefficients and coordinate scalings that collapse distinct knots now raise numerical-failure exceptions. Monotone interpolation scales derivatives before limiting them on narrow intervals.
- QR scales large responses before reflector products. Decimal PCA recovers representable tiny standard deviations, and its legacy component selector preserves thresholds that round down during decimal conversion.
- Jacobi includes both triangles in its off-diagonal stopping norm and refuses overflowing eigenvalues. Pivoted Cholesky uses round-to-nearest unit roundoff for its default rank cutoff.
- Numerical documentation now distinguishes truncated Cholesky residuals, residual-dependent least-squares sensitivity, numerical PSD acceptance, and seed-dependent elimination decisions. Higham projection does not promise exact PSD or strict Cholesky acceptance.

- Brent distinguishes coincident interpolation points by exact equality, preserving valid inverse-quadratic steps when the abscissas use very small units.

- Nelder–Mead rejects invalid coefficient ranges and negative tolerances before evaluating the objective. Stable temporal ordering preserves existing equal-valued vertices and the old best after shrink, following the governing tie rules.

- Muller repeats the previous increment when its quadratic denominator is zero and repeatedly halves trials whose residual grows by more than tenfold. Non-finite trial residuals backtrack from finite seeds. Documentation now states the real-root scope; this API does not return complex roots.

- LM central finite differences use the cube-root machine-epsilon scale and represented parameter spacing. The corrected stencil intentionally changes historical floating-point trajectories; independent derivative and fit checks replace those obsolete bit expectations.
- LM recognizes small actual and predicted reductions on rejected trials without accepting their parameters, avoiding spurious damping-overflow termination at rounded cost floors. Clipped steps use their actual quadratic-model reduction; legacy null initial guesses retain `ArgumentNullException`.

- Wide finite DSR and MinTRL inputs no longer lose representable results to intermediate overflow; Haircut Student-t tails retain their normal limit at very large finite observation counts.
- MP denoising averages the all-noise spectrum; detoning protects long Half panels and tiny decimal residuals. QIS normalizes its trace without overflow, POET converts its threshold to Bessel units consistently, and Tracy-Widom centering/scaling reverses Wishart dimensions for wide matrices.
- Hand-built GARCH forecast records reject invalid numeric state. Graphical-lasso reuses column buffers within each solve. Independent forecast, weighted-penalty, MinTRL, PSD-oracle and long-Half covariance assertions cover these contracts.

- `OrdinaryLeastSquares<decimal>` normalises every Householder reflector. `decimal`'s fixed 28-place scale resolves an absolute step, so a raw reflector on a column far from unit magnitude lost most of its digits: NIST Pontius returned coefficients 2% off with a residual sum of squares above the certified minimum, and a fit of data scaled by 1e-12 kept fewer than 5 correct digits. Every NIST StRD problem except Filip now matches the certified coefficients to 1e-13 at `T = decimal` (Filip 1e-6, limited by its 15-digit tabulated inputs), and the decimal NIST tests assert those bars.
- `OrdinaryLeastSquares<Half>` sizes its reflector-normalisation band from `Half`'s own range. The band was derived from `decimal.MaxValue`, which is `+∞` in `Half`, so no reflector was normalised and designs whose column norms square past 65504 overflowed to NaN or were rejected as rank-deficient.
- `OrdinaryLeastSquares<T>` for non-`decimal` `T`: the augmented-system refinement stores the residual iterate at its own binary exponent when forming `g = −Xᵀr`, instead of at the response's. At the response's scale the tiny `g` of a near-converged iterate kept only `decimal`'s fixed absolute resolution, which `(XᵀX)⁻¹` amplifies by `1/σ_min²`, so the error on consistent ill-conditioned systems grew as κ² (1.3e-6 at κ ≈ 3·10¹¹). Such systems are now recovered exactly.
- Documentation: `OrdinaryLeastSquares<T>` and `OlsResult<T>` state the accuracy the NIST StRD tests assert (double: 1e-12 on coefficients and 1e-11 on standard errors except Filip at 1e-7 and 1e-6; decimal: 1e-13 except Filip at 1e-6) instead of a uniform 10-digit claim that Filip missed and a "uniform 28-digit precision" promise for `decimal`. The legacy `OrdinaryLeastSquares` facade's obsolete message no longer promises "28-digit QR".
- `DistanceCorrelation<T>.Compute` returns `0` instead of `NaN` when the empirical joint distribution is a product of its marginals (for example a factorial design where every x-level meets every y-level). The distance covariance is exactly zero there, and rounding could leave it a tiny negative number whose square root was `NaN`; the result now stays in the documented `[0, 1]` range.
- `PolitisWhiteBlockLength.EstimateDouble` searches for the lag window up to Patton's horizon `⌈√T⌉ + K_N` with `K_N = max(5, √log₁₀T)`, falls back to the largest significant lag, and caps the result at `⌈min(3√T, T/3)⌉`. The former `max(5, ⌈5·log₁₀T⌉)` horizon truncated the lag window of persistent series: for AR(1) with φ = 0.95 at T = 5000 the mean estimate was about 59 against a paper-rule 109 and a theoretical optimum of 124. The window was also six lags instead of five for T above 100 000, and the result was clamped at T/2.
- `FastDoubleBootstrap` gains `PValueImposingNull`, which takes a transform that imposes the null hypothesis on a sample and applies it to the data and to every outer sample before its inner draw, as the fast double bootstrap requires (Davidson 2023 §4). `PValue` resamples raw data at both levels, which is valid only for a statistic invariant to the parameters the null restricts; its documentation now says so, and the README example, statistics guide and verification golden use the null-imposed form. For `|mean|` on N(0, 1) samples of size 20, the raw-inner configuration rejected about 17% of the time at nominal 5%; the null-imposed form rejects about 5–6%.
- `LevenbergMarquardtSolver<T>.Solve` (all overloads) no longer throws when a bounded parameter's optimum lies on `lowerBounds`/`upperBounds`: the finite-difference Jacobian previously perturbed `theta ± h` without regard to the caller's bounds, so an accepted iterate sitting exactly on a bound could evaluate the residual function just outside its declared domain and throw `InvalidOperationException` from the very next Jacobian evaluation. `FiniteDifferenceJacobian<T>.EvaluateInto` now skips a perturbation direction that would leave the box and falls back to the existing one-sided-difference formula toward the interior.
- `JacobiEigenDecomposition<T>.Decompose` converges to near working precision instead of stopping at an off-diagonal Frobenius-norm residual of up to ~3.16e-8 (the convergence check compared against `sqrt(tolerance)` where the rest of the file compares tolerance-scaled magnitudes directly). Reconstruction error on well-conditioned inputs is now on the order of 1e-15 instead of 1e-8; `JacobiEigenDecomposition<decimal>` can now measurably out-converge `JacobiEigenDecomposition<double>` on the same input, as documented, instead of hitting an identical residual regardless of `T`. This also fixes two downstream defects that shared the same root cause: `NearestPsdProjection<T>.EigenClip` no longer perturbs an already positive-definite input by up to ~3e-8 relative, and `NearestPsdProjection<T>.Higham` no longer spuriously throws "did not converge" on an input that is already a valid correlation matrix (the prior residual noise floor exceeded the default 1e-12 convergence tolerance).
- `NearestPsdProjection<T>.IsPsd(T[,], T)` now throws `ArgumentException` on a non-square matrix, matching its siblings `EigenClip` and `Higham`, instead of silently returning `false` and hiding a malformed-matrix bug behind a "not PSD" result.
- `NearestPsdProjection<T>.IsPsd(T[,])` (the default-tolerance overload) now scales its default tolerance by the matrix's own magnitude, so an exactly-PSD matrix (e.g. a rank-1 outer product) at scale above ~10 is no longer misreported as non-PSD by an absolute -1e-12 tolerance that does not absorb magnitude-proportional floating-point rounding. The explicit-tolerance overload `IsPsd(T[,], T)` is unchanged; only the convenience default is affected.
- `NelderMeadMinimizer<T>.Minimize` now rejects a non-positive `SimplexOptions<T>.MaxIterations` with `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`, matching every sibling solver in the family (`BisectionSolver<T>`, `BrentSolver<T>`, `SecantSolver<T>`, `MullerSolver<T>`, `NewtonRaphsonSolver<T>`, `LevenbergMarquardtSolver<T>`). It previously returned a normal-looking `Converged = false` result at the initial simplex instead of a diagnostic exception.
- `LevenbergMarquardtSolver<T>.Solve` now requires both the actual AND the model's predicted relative cost reduction within `functionTolerance` before reporting `FunctionToleranceReached`, matching MINPACK `lmder`'s ftol convention more closely than the actual-reduction-only check this replaces. A step whose cost happened to land near its starting magnitude (equal residual magnitude, opposite sign) but whose model still predicted substantial further improvement was previously reported as converged at a non-stationary point.
- `DeflatedSharpeRatio<T>` now follows Bailey & López de Prado (2014) eqs. 1–2. The annualized Sharpe is converted to the per-period frequency of the returns before it meets the sample length T, the rejection threshold is `SR̂₀ = √V·((1−γ)Z⁻¹[1−1/N] + γZ⁻¹[1−e⁻¹/N])` rather than a Gumbel approximation with no variance term, and the standard error uses `√(T−1)`. The p-value no longer changes when only the periods-per-year label changes, and the paper's worked example (annualized SR 2.5, five years of daily data, N = 100, V = 1/2, skewness −3, kurtosis 10) now gives DSR 0.9004 instead of 0.005. New `ComputeWithTrialVariance` takes the variance of the trials' Sharpe ratios; `Compute` and `ComputeFromReturns` use its null value 1/(T−1). `ExpectedMaxSharpe` is SR̂₀ in annualized units and `PValue` is 1 − DSR.
- `MinimumTrackRecordLength<T>` divides the annualized Sharpe ratios by √`tradingDaysPerYear` before applying Bailey & López de Prado (2012) eq. 13, so the result is a count of per-period observations. An annualized Sharpe of 2 against a benchmark of 1 with daily data now needs 688.2 observations (2.73 years), the paper's value, instead of 9.1.
- Tests: the DSR and MinTRL verification goldens are regenerated from the papers' equations rather than a port of the C# formulas, and both papers' worked examples are pinned.
- `LedoitWolfShrinkageEstimator<T>` now uses the Ledoit-Wolf (2004, JMVA) intensity `δ = min(b̄², d²)/d²` (eq. 14). It no longer subtracts a diagonal "ρ" term, which under-shrank by about 20%. Results match `sklearn.covariance.ledoit_wolf`, rescaled to the library's T−1 covariance. `DenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>` uses this estimator for fewer than three assets when shrinkage is enabled, so that path changes too.
- `LedoitWolfConstantCorrelationEstimator<T>` now uses the "Honey, I Shrunk the Sample Covariance Matrix" Appendix B intensity `max(0, min((π̂ − ρ̂)/(T·γ̂), 1))`, including the ρ̂ cross terms. The previous library-specific formula over-shrank.
- `LedoitWolfSingleFactorEstimator<T>` now uses the Ledoit-Wolf (2003) Theorem 2 intensity `(p − r)/(c·T)` with Lemma 2's `r`. Leaving out `r` over-shrank the estimate and could clip the intensity to 1.
- `QuadraticInverseShrinkageEstimator<T>` now implements the Ledoit-Wolf (2022) QIS algorithm from the paper's Appendix A. It works on the covariance with `n = T − 1`, uses the bandwidth `min(c², 1/c²)^0.35 / N^0.35` and the smoothed Stein shrinker, gives null eigenvalues `1/((c − 1)·mean(1/λ))` when N > T − 1, and preserves the trace. The previous correlation-matrix transform was not the published estimator. It could return non-PSD matrices with a larger Frobenius error than the sample covariance.
- In all four estimators, the paper intensities are computed on 1/T moments. The returned matrices stay on the library's Bessel (T − 1) scale, which is `T/(T − 1)` times the papers' estimators.
- Documentation: `BrentSolver<T>` no longer claims bisection's worst case. Its forced-bisection safeguard halves the bracket at least every two iterations, so the documented worst case is `2⌈log₂((b−a)/tol)⌉ + 2` iterations, now asserted on three stalling functions. The halving paper formerly cited for the bound (Steffen, Della Pasqua, de Oliveira & da Silva, 2025) states no iteration bound; it is now cited correctly, as related work. The description of Brent's method no longer lists golden-section search, which belongs to Brent's minimizer.
- Documentation: the `LevenbergMarquardtSolver<T>` damping rule now states Nielsen's reset `ν ← 2` after an accepted step and that `ν` starts at 2, matching both the 1999 report and the shipped code.
- Documentation: the NIST StRD nonlinear-regression suite has 27 problems, all tested; the count read 26. The solver guide also had Lanczos3 and Lanczos1/2 in each other's NIST difficulty tiers.
- Tests: the Brent halving test no longer runs on a bracket symmetric about the root, where the first step lands on the root and the safeguard is never exercised.
- `BrentSolver<T>` stops as Brent's `zero` does: on a bracket half-width within `2ε|b| + tolerance/2`, or when `f` is exactly zero. It no longer accepts a point, including a bracket endpoint, because `|f|` is below the tolerance. On `f(x) = 1e-8·(x − 0.5)` over `[0, 1]` with tolerance 1e-6 it returned `x = 0` as converged; it now returns 0.5.
- `SecantSolver<T>` no longer reports `Converged = true` for a step below the tolerance unless the residual also changes sign across that step. On `cosh`, which has no real root, it reported convergence with residuals between 1.2 and 6.2.
- `MullerSolver<T>` uses the relative step `|x₃ − x₂| / |x₃| < tolerance` from Muller (1956, p. 210) when the new iterate is nonzero and the interpolating quadratic has a real root. The negative-discriminant `sqrt(abs(disc))` continuation cannot establish this convergence criterion; at zero or after that continuation, only the residual test applies. A small relative step does not guarantee a small residual.
- The bracketed `NewtonRaphsonSolver<T>.Solve` without an analytic derivative keeps its finite-difference stencil inside `[lowerBound, upperBound]`, using a one-sided difference at an edge. On brackets whose edge is a domain boundary (`√x − 1e-3` on `[0, 1]`, `log x + 20` on `[1e-12, 1]`) it evaluated the objective at a negative argument and threw `InvalidOperationException`; Brent and bisection solve the same brackets.
- The bracketed `NewtonRaphsonSolver<T>.Solve` recognizes representational stagnation. Bisection now follows strict signed-step halving as described above; its earlier adjacent-endpoint shortcut has been superseded.
- `NewtonRaphsonSolver<T>` without an analytic derivative uses a central-difference step of `∛ε` for `T`, replacing a fixed 1e-8 that is below `float` resolution and rounds to zero at `Half`. Derivative-free Newton at `float` and `Half` failed at the first iteration and now converges.
- The bracketed `NewtonRaphsonSolver<T>.Solve` adds `rtsafe`'s slow-decrease bisection test, `|2f| > |dxold·f′|` (Numerical Recipes, 3rd ed., §9.4). On `(x − 0.3)⁹` over `[0, 1]` it no longer needs more than four times bisection's iteration count.
- `OrdinaryLeastSquares<T>` for non-`decimal` `T`: the residual sum of squares, standard errors, covariance and R² no longer collapse to `0`, `0` and `1` when the data are small (residuals below ~1e-14): the RSS is now accumulated in `T` from the residuals, which are evaluated in `decimal` after an exact power-of-two rescaling. Inputs now enter the `decimal` residual through their exact binary value rather than a 15-significant-digit conversion, so an exactly consistent system has zero residual. Refinement now uses Björck's augmented-system method, which corrects the residual together with the coefficients; correcting the coefficients alone reached `κ·u` only on nearly consistent systems. NIST Wampler4 and Wampler5 coefficients are now recovered exactly; the per-problem test bars are unchanged.
- `OrdinaryLeastSquares<T>` no longer reports a full-rank design with small-magnitude columns (for example a single regressor of order 1e-16) as rank-deficient, and no longer accepts a design whose columns are exactly dependent at a large magnitude. The Householder QR rank test is now relative to each column's own norm (`max(m, p)·ε`), replacing an absolute floor of `2/decimal.MaxValue`, and reflectors for columns far below unit magnitude are normalized so they cannot underflow.
- `HaircutSharpe<T>` now follows Harvey & Liu (2015). The t-ratio of the annualized Sharpe is `SR · √backTestYears`; it was `SR · √(backTestYears · tradingDaysPerYear)`, which overstated significance by `√tradingDaysPerYear` and made the result depend on the calendar label. The single-test p-value is two-sided on a Student t with `T − 1` degrees of freedom; it was a one-sided normal. `HaircutSharpe` is the Bonferroni-adjusted Sharpe of the paper's eq. 5, so a single trial gets no haircut and a negative Sharpe shrinks toward zero. `BhyPValue` applies the BHY step-up factor `M · c(M)` for the top-ranked strategy; it was `c(M)`. On the paper's worked example (annual Sharpe 0.75, 240 months, 200 trials) the haircut Sharpe is 0.297; it was 0.513, against the paper's 0.32. A backtest of one observation or fewer now throws `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`. The verification vectors are now derived from the paper's equations with `scipy.stats.t` and include its worked example; they were a port of the C# formula.
- `PoetCovarianceEstimator<T>` now thresholds residual entries at Fan, Liao & Mincheva's (2013) adaptive level `τ_ij = C·√θ̂_ij·(1/√N + √(log N / T))` (eq. 3.2), where `θ̂_ij` is the variance of `û_it·û_jt` over the factor-model residuals. The former fixed `τ = c·√(log N / T)` had no data scale: at daily-return scale it zeroed every residual off-diagonal and collapsed POET to the strict factor model. The estimator is now scale-equivariant. This changes POET output (and the decimal facade's) for every input with a non-zero multiplier.
- `PoetCovarianceEstimator<T>()` and `PoetCovarianceEstimator<T>(numFactors)` now use the documented 0.5 threshold multiplier, and `DoublySparseEstimator<T>()` the documented 0.1 eigenvector threshold. With an unconstrained `T`, the omitted `T?` argument was `0`, so the default POET returned the sample covariance and the default doubly-sparse estimator did no eigenvector sparsification. New parameterless (and POET `numFactors`-only) constructors carry the documented defaults.
- Covariance estimators at `T = Half` no longer return NaN when one observation sits far from the rest: the per-asset mean is now always reduced in `double` and rounded once, instead of an anchored `Half` deviation sum that overflowed `Half.MaxValue` when the outlier was in the first row.
- Tests: the covariance verification theories for QIS, Denoised, Detoned, Tracy-Widom and doubly-sparse are renamed `*_MatchesPortedPipeline`, because their goldens port this library's pipelines and cannot show paper fidelity. The POET golden is now derived from the paper rather than ported, and keeps the `Poet_MatchesPaperReference` name.
- `DetonedCovarianceEstimator<T>()` now detones fully (alpha = 1) as documented. A parameterless constructor replaces the nullable-default path, which gave alpha = 0 for every value type and returned the denoised estimate.
- `DetonedCovarianceEstimator<T>` removes the market component (the first eigenpair of the denoised correlation, scaled by alpha) and rescales to a unit diagonal, per López de Prado (2020) section 2.6. It previously shrank the PC1 eigenvalue toward the mean of the other signal eigenvalues and kept the matrix full rank. At alpha = 1, removing a market component with a positive residual diagonal yields a singular result; small-asset, all-noise and zero-residual fallback paths need not be singular. With no signal eigenvalue the complete noise spectrum is averaged, without market-component removal.
- `DenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `DetonedCovarianceEstimator<T>` and `TracyWidomDenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>` rescale the cleaned correlation to a unit diagonal (`C_ij / sqrt(C_ii·C_jj)`, clipped to [−1, 1]) instead of overwriting its diagonal with 1. Off-diagonal entries change whenever cleaning is applied, and the result stays PSD.
- `TracyWidomDenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>` uses Johnstone's (2001) 99% threshold `mu_np/T + (sigma_np/T)·2.0234`. It previously centered at the Marčenko-Pastur edge and multiplied the 99th percentile by the magnitude of the Tracy-Widom mean, which tested at about the 99.5% level.
- `FastDoubleBootstrap.PValue` with `PValueTail.RightTail` (the default) took the `p*`-quantile of the inner distribution instead of the `(1 − p*)`-quantile that Davidson-MacKinnon require for a right-rejecting test, so it returned roughly `1 − p`: a strong rejection read as p ≈ 1. `TwoSided` now returns `min(2·min(p_right, p_left), 1)` of the two corrected one-sided FDB p-values. `LeftTail` is unchanged. Seeded p-values change.
- `PSquareEstimator<T>` marker updates no longer overflow when adjacent markers straddle zero with a gap wider than `T`'s range: `double` estimates could become `-∞`/`NaN` permanently and `decimal` streams threw `OverflowException`. The parabolic step is now evaluated at quarter scale.
- `PolitisWhiteBlockLength.EstimateDouble` now sums signed autocorrelations in `G` (Politis-White 2004 eq. 8) instead of their absolute values, and divides by `D̂_SB = 2·ĝ(0)²` (Patton-Politis-White 2009). Negatively autocorrelated series were inflated about threefold, and every estimate by `2^{1/3}`.
- `Pcg64RandomSource` now permutes the advanced state (step, then output), matching the pcg-c and pcg-cpp pcg64 sequences; draws were one step out of phase. Every seeded sequence changes, including seeded bootstrap and sampler results.
- `ProbabilityOfBacktestOverfitting<T>` now follows Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2014) §2: each strategy's Sharpe ratio is computed on the joined S/2 training blocks and on the joined test blocks, not averaged over per-block Sharpe ratios. Strategies tied with the in-sample winner out-of-sample share the average rank of their tie group; the winner used to take the lowest rank, so adding an exact copy of a winning strategy turned PBO 0 into PBO 1. A constant return series now always has Sharpe 0; summation rounding used to give it a Sharpe near 1e16 for some block lengths. PBO values change for every panel. The verification golden is now written from the paper with numpy and `scipy.stats.rankdata`; it was a port of the C# selection rule.
- `GtInspiredScore<T>` takes z = 0 (the penalty branch) for every constant return series. When 1/N was not exactly representable, the rounding residue of the summed mean gave a standard error near 1e-18 and z near 1e16, so a constant series scored as the best possible strategy for most lengths.
- Documentation: `ActiveSetQpSolver<T>` takes a `T[,]` covariance matrix; the statistics guide said it consumed `ICovarianceEstimator<T>`.
- `MonotoneCubicInterpolator<T>` and `MonotoneCubicSpline<T>` now initialise tangents as Fritsch & Carlson (1980) §4 Step 1 prescribes. Interior tangents use the three-point formula `(hᵢ·δᵢ₋₁ + hᵢ₋₁·δᵢ)/(hᵢ₋₁ + hᵢ)` instead of the arithmetic mean of the adjacent secants, and a one-sided end estimate with the wrong sign becomes 0 instead of the end secant. Monotone quadratics are now reproduced exactly on non-uniform grids (on `x²` over nodes 0, 1, 3 the value at 2 was 4.125 and is now 4). At `T = decimal`, steep but finite slopes no longer throw `OverflowException`: the sign tests compare signs instead of multiplying slopes.
- `CubicSplineInterpolator<T>` no longer returns NaN (IEEE types) or throws `OverflowException` (`decimal`) when finite ordinates are large enough to overflow the tridiagonal right-hand side, for example natural-spline ordinates of ±1e307 in `double` or ±1e28 in `decimal`. The spline is linear in the ordinates, so such inputs are solved on data normalized by their magnitude and rescaled; inputs that did not overflow keep the unnormalized computation.
- `MonotoneConvexInterpolator<T>` now applies Hagan & West's (2006) monotonicity and convexity sectors (eqs. 45–56) after the positivity bounds. It used the basic quadratic on every interval, so strictly increasing input forwards could produce a forward curve that dips below every input: with forwards 1.0%, 1.1%, 3.0% the forward on the second interval fell to 0.78%. The paper's flat segment now holds it at 1.05% there. NCR values change wherever an interval falls in sectors (ii)–(iv); on the verification yield curve the NCR at 3 years moves from 0.106073 to 0.107595. Tests: the Hagan-West verification golden is regenerated from the paper's piecewise forward, integrated numerically, instead of a port of the previous C# formula.
- `MonotoneCubicInterpolator<T>` and `MonotoneCubicSpline<T>` no longer overshoot on decreasing data when the Fritsch-Carlson circle rescales a tangent. The rescale multiplied by the signed secant slope, which flipped the tangents' sign on decreasing intervals; on nodes (0, 0), (1, −1), (2, −10) the value at 0.6 was −1.08, outside [−1, 0].
- `CholeskyDecomposition<T>.DecomposePivoted(a)` and the `CholeskyDecomposition.DecomposePivoted` legacy facade's default now apply Higham's (1990) recommended relative stopping rule (eq. 4.3: `ε·â₁₁⁽¹⁾` with `ε = n·u`) instead of an absolute zero tolerance. The previous default admitted floating-point rounding residue on a rank-deficient input as a spurious extra pivot — a 4×4 exact-rank-2 matrix returned Rank 3 with a third diagonal on the order of the residue itself (`1.2e-08`), and across 200 random 8×8 rank-3 matrices the default overestimated rank in 186. Passing an explicit `tolerance` is unaffected.
- Tests: a new `SelectPivotRow` test exercises `GaussianElimination<T>`'s randomized-pivoting mechanism directly (via a two-eligible-candidate column across many seeds), because every existing test asserted only the seed-invariant final solution and so passed even when randomization was disabled entirely.
- Documentation: `GaussianElimination<T>`'s XML remarks no longer claim the randomized top-50% pivot heuristic stays within partial pivoting's stability floor or that seeds always agree numerically. The heuristic allows multipliers up to 2 (partial pivoting bounds them at 1) and a growth factor up to `3ⁿ⁻¹` (audited: 9841.5 at n=10 vs 512 for partial pivoting); on ill-scaled inputs, different seeds can pick different eligible pivots and diverge, including one seed overflowing and another succeeding.
- `WelfordMoments<T>.Add` and `Merge` no longer drop an observation's (or a partition's) contribution to the running M2 when the raw difference from the current mean overflows `T`. The overflow fallback now adds that contribution in scaled form, so `{-1e308, 1e308}` reports a `double` variance of +Infinity (the true 2e616 is not representable) instead of 0, and the `decimal` variance getter throws `OverflowException`, as the non-fallback scaled path already does, instead of returning 0.
- `WelfordMoments<T>.Pearson` normalizes x and y by their own magnitudes once it switches to scaled accumulation, and its overflow fallback now adds the pair's M2 and co-moment contribution. A shared scale set by the larger series underflowed the smaller series' second moment, so `Correlation` returned 0 (or a wrong value) where the batch `PearsonCorrelation<T>` was correct, e.g. x of order 1e200 against y of order 1 in `double`, or x of order 1e15 against y of order 1e-3 in `decimal`.
- `SampleExcessKurtosis<T>.Compute` normalizes by the largest absolute observation before its Welford pass, as `SampleSkewness<T>.Compute` already does. Kurtosis is scale-invariant, but it returned -5.4 for `double` data scaled by 1e200 and threw `OverflowException` for `decimal` data of order 1e14 and above.
- `JacobiEigenDecomposition<T>.Decompose` no longer stalls and throws "did not converge" on large small-magnitude matrices (e.g. a 30-asset covariance at 0.1% daily volatility). The per-pair rotation-skip floor is now the convergence threshold divided by `n`, so skipped off-diagonals can no longer keep the off-diagonal norm above the threshold. `PrincipalComponentAnalysis<T>`, `NearestPsdProjection<T>.IsPsd` and the covariance estimators that decompose unnormalized matrices inherit the fix.
- `JacobiEigenDecomposition<T>.Decompose` now throws `ArgumentException` on a NaN or infinite entry in the upper triangle it reads, matching `CholeskyDecomposition<T>` and `GaussianElimination<T>`, instead of running 100 sweeps and reporting non-convergence. `NearestPsdProjection<T>.EigenClip`/`IsPsd` and `PrincipalComponentAnalysis<T>.Decompose` inherit the check.
- `GaussianElimination<T>.Solve` now throws the documented `InvalidOperationException` when back substitution overflows on a near-singular system, instead of returning ±Infinity (IEEE types) or leaking `OverflowException` (`decimal`, including the legacy facade).
- `CholeskyDecomposition<T>.Decompose` and `DecomposePivoted` at `T = decimal` (and the legacy `CholeskyDecomposition` facade) now throw the documented `InvalidOperationException` on non-positive-definite input that overflows the factor, instead of leaking `OverflowException`.
- `CubicSplineInterpolator<T>` now solves on abscissae scaled by a power of `T`'s radix near the widest knot spacing. `decimal` splines no longer lose curvature on wide spacings (from about 1e8, e.g. Unix-millisecond knots), and `double` not-a-knot, modified not-a-knot and Q-spline boundaries no longer return NaN at spacings of about 1e155 and above. Radix-power scaling is exact, so `double` results on ordinary grids are unchanged.
- `PoetCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `DoublySparseEstimator<T>` and `NercomeCovarianceEstimator<T>` now constrain `T` to `struct`, so their optional `T?` parameters are `Nullable<T>` and an explicit `default` argument selects the documented default. Before, `new PoetCovarianceEstimator<double>(1, default)` passed a multiplier of 0 and returned the sample covariance, `new DoublySparseEstimator<double>(default)` skipped eigenvector sparsification, and `new NercomeCovarianceEstimator<double>(default)` threw `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`. Hidden plain-`T` constructor overloads keep binaries compiled against earlier versions working. Generic code that instantiates these types with a type parameter not constrained to `struct` must add the constraint.
- `QuadraticInverseShrinkageEstimator<T>` at `T = Half` no longer returns an all-NaN matrix for long samples (for example 4 assets and 60,000 observations). The `c²·|θ|²` term is formed as `(c·θ)² + (c·Hθ)²`, so `Hθ²` no longer overflows while `c²` underflows. Results for `double`, `float` and `decimal` change only by rounding.
- Documentation: the `ICovarianceEstimator` PSD contract now describes the current algorithms. Denoised and Tracy-Widom output is PSD, since the unit-diagonal rescale preserves it. Detoned output is PSD up to rounding; full market-component removal with a positive residual diagonal is singular, while fallback paths need not be. Skewness and kurtosis are now computed on deviations normalized by their maximum absolute value — scale-free by construction — instead of on raw fourth-order products that underflowed to zero while the standard deviation itself stayed representable. - `MinimumTrackRecordLength<T>.Compute` — `T significanceLevel` parameter changed to `double significanceLevel`. Same rationale as `Percentile.Compute<T>`.
- `PcaResult<T>.NumComponentsForExplainedVariance` — `T threshold` parameter changed to `double threshold`. The decimal facade overload is updated in parallel. Same rationale as `Percentile.Compute<T>`.
- `GtInspiredScore<T>` — redesigned from a configurable weighted additive statistic into an explicitly named GT-inspired library piecewise search loss (lower is better). `Compute` now accepts a return span plus optional benchmark mean and epsilon; `GtInspiredScoreResult<T>` exposes `Score`, `Mu`, `Z`, `RSquared`, and `DownsideDeviation`. The old sub-period/trading-day/weight parameters and four weighted result components are removed. The generic type is now Tier B (`IFloatingPointIeee754<T>`), and the legacy facade follows the new shape.
- **Span-first input parameters.** Every method that previously accepted a read-only array now accepts `ReadOnlySpan<T>` (with `default` for optional bounds). Affected types include all bootstrap resamplers, `Subsampler.Run<T>` and its statistic callback, `BootstrapMonteCarloEngine<T>.Run/RunMulti` and their callbacks, `FastDoubleBootstrap.PValue`, both percentile estimators, `PolitisWhiteBlockLength.EstimateDouble`, OLS, ActiveSet mean-variance, backtest statistics, rolling correlation, and Levenberg-Marquardt/interfaces. Arrays still convert implicitly; callers passing `null` for optional bounds must omit the argument or use `default`.
- `CubicSplineInterpolator<T>` and `MonotoneCubicInterpolator<T>` — generic constraint tightened from `IFloatingPoint<T>` to `unmanaged, IFloatingPoint<T>` to support `stackalloc T[n]` internal buffers. All practical `IFloatingPoint<T>` types (`double`, `float`, `Half`, `decimal`) satisfy `unmanaged`; callers instantiated at those types compile unchanged. Third-party `IFloatingPoint<T>` implementations that are managed (class or struct containing reference fields) are now rejected at compile time with `CS0315`.
- `InterpolatorFactory<T>` — constraint tightened from `IFloatingPoint<T>` to `unmanaged, IFloatingPoint<T>` to match `CubicSplineInterpolator<T>` and `MonotoneCubicInterpolator<T>`.

### Removed

- `PolitisWhiteBlockLength.Estimate<T>(T[] series)` — the generic wrapper immediately cast to `double`. Call `PolitisWhiteBlockLength.EstimateDouble(ReadOnlySpan<double>)` instead.
- `PearsonCorrelation.Compute(decimal[] x, decimal[] y, int start, int end)` and `PearsonCorrelation<T>.Compute(T[] x, T[] y, int start, int end)` — the indexed windowed overloads taking explicit `start`/`end` offsets have been removed. Use `PearsonCorrelation<T>.Compute(x.AsSpan(start, end - start), y.AsSpan(start, end - start))` or `PearsonCorrelation<T>.Rolling(returnsA, returnsB, windowSize)` instead.

### Deprecated

52 legacy concrete-typed facades are marked `[Obsolete(DiagnosticId = "BNU0001")]` (warning, not error). They remain fully functional in 2.x and will be removed in v3.0. Migrate by appending the appropriate `<T>` suffix (see [`docs/migration-to-generic-math.md`](docs/migration-to-generic-math.md) for before/after examples and suppression options).

**Solvers** — `BrentSolver`, `BisectionSolver`, `NewtonRaphsonSolver`, `SecantSolver`, `MullerSolver`, `LevenbergMarquardtSolver` (all `<double>`); `OrdinaryLeastSquares` (`<double>` or `<decimal>`); `ActiveSetQpSolver` (`<decimal>`).

**Linear algebra** — `CholeskyDecomposition`, `GaussianElimination`, `JacobiEigenDecomposition`, `NearestPsdProjection`, `PrincipalComponentAnalysis` (all `<decimal>`).

**Distributions** — `CumulativeNormal`, `InverseNormal`, `NormalDistribution` (all `<double>`).

**Interpolation** — `LinearInterpolator`, `LogLinearInterpolator`, `FlatForwardInterpolator`, `MonotoneCubicInterpolator`, `MonotoneConvexInterpolator`, `CubicSplineInterpolator`, `MonotoneCubicSpline`, `TwoPointLinearInterpolator`, `InterpolatorFactory` (all `<double>`).

**Statistics** — `WelfordMoments`, `ReturnsMatrix`, `PearsonCorrelation`, `SampleSkewness`, `SampleExcessKurtosis` (`<decimal>`); `SampleCovarianceEstimator`, `LedoitWolfShrinkageEstimator`, `LedoitWolfConstantCorrelationEstimator`, `LedoitWolfSingleFactorEstimator`, `OracleApproximatingShrinkageEstimator`, `QuadraticInverseShrinkageEstimator`, `DenoisedCovarianceEstimator`, `DetonedCovarianceEstimator`, `TracyWidomDenoisedCovarianceEstimator`, `NercomeCovarianceEstimator`, `PoetCovarianceEstimator`, `DoublySparseEstimator`, `ExponentiallyWeightedCovarianceEstimator` (`<decimal>`); `RankCorrelation`, `DistanceCorrelation`, `FisherZTransform`, `HaircutSharpe`, `DeflatedSharpeRatio`, `NeweyWestVariance`, `GtInspiredScore`, `MinimumTrackRecordLength`, `ProbabilityOfBacktestOverfitting` (`<double>`).

### Performance

- `RollingWindow<T>.ToArray` — rewritten from an indexer-driven copy loop (one `%` modulo per element) to two `Array.Copy` calls keyed off a single `(head − count + Capacity) % Capacity` start position. Hot-path Monte Carlo producers that materialise the window on every tick see the per-call cost drop from O(n) modulos to O(1) modulos.
- `BootstrapMonteCarloEngine<T>.RunMulti` — replaced `Enumerable.Range(0, statisticCount).Select(i => $"stat_{i}").ToArray()` with a pre-sized `string[]` populated by a single `for` loop; saves the LINQ enumerator/iterator allocation on every invocation where caller-supplied `names` are `null`.
- `ActiveSetQpSolver<T>.SolveMaxReturnLP` — replaced `Enumerable.Range(0, n).OrderByDescending(i => means[i]).ToArray()` with an in-place `Array.Sort(int[], Comparison<int>)` index sort. One array allocation, no LINQ pipeline.
- `NercomeCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `TracyWidomDenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `PoetCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `QuadraticInverseShrinkageEstimator<T>` — share a single `private static readonly SampleCovarianceEstimator<T> s_sampleEstimator = new();` instance instead of allocating a fresh stateless estimator on every `Estimate` call, matching the pattern already used in `DenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>`.
- `HarrellDavisPercentile.Compute<T>` — the Beta-weights vector is now allocated via `stackalloc double[n]` when `n ≤ 512` (≤ 4 KB on the thread stack), falling back to `new double[n]` for larger inputs. The six Lanczos coefficients in the internal `LogGamma` helper are promoted to a `private static readonly double[]` field, eliminating one heap allocation per `LogGamma` call. Together these changes bring per-call managed-heap allocation to zero for the common small-sample case — verified by `BootstrapAllocationTests.HarrellDavisPercentile_ZeroHeapAllocation_WhenNLe512`.
- `CubicSplineInterpolator<T>` — the four `ThomasSolve` scratch vectors (diagonal, upper, lower, RHS of the tridiagonal system) are now allocated via `stackalloc T[n]` when `n ≤ 256` across all boundary-condition solvers (`SolveNatural`, `SolveClamped`, `SolveNotAKnot`, `SolveModifiedNotAKnot`, `SolveQSpline`). The `ThomasSolve` private helper is updated to accept `Span<T>` parameters; `T[]` implicitly converts to `Span<T>` at all existing call sites.
- `MonotoneCubicInterpolator<T>` — the `delta` (size `n − 1`) and `m` (size `n`) tangent vectors are now allocated via `stackalloc T[n]` when the relevant size is ≤ 256, eliminating two heap allocations per `Interpolate` call on typical node sets.

## [1.0.0] — 2026-04-16

First public release. Boutquin.Numerics ships a generic-math numerical methods library for .NET 10: the caller picks the working type (`double`, `float`, `Half`, `decimal`), and every algorithm is parameterised over `T` at the tier its critical path actually needs. Legacy concrete-typed public APIs are kept live as forwarding facades for pre-generic-math consumers.

### Highlights

- **Generic-math surface across the library.** `BrentSolver<T>`, `CholeskyDecomposition<T>`, `OrdinaryLeastSquares<T>`, and every other algorithm accept the caller's choice of `T`. The tier model — Tier A (arithmetic), Tier A + √ (plus `Sqrt` via the internal `NumericPrecision<T>` dispatcher), Tier B (transcendentals, IEEE only), Tier C (polynomial-approximation-bound) — is documented in [`docs/tier-constraints.md`](docs/tier-constraints.md). Tier B on `decimal` is a future enhancement; all other tiers accept `decimal`.
- **Decimal-precision OLS at `T = decimal`.** `OrdinaryLeastSquares<decimal>` runs the full QR factorisation, `Qᵀ·y`, upper-triangular solve, and `(XᵀX)⁻¹` in 28-digit `decimal` on every one of the 11 NIST StRD linear-regression problems. The current decimal coefficient gate is 1e-13 on ten problems and 1e-6 on Filip. Wampler4 and Wampler5 share the same degree-five design (condition number about 6.4·10⁶) with different response noise. `T = double` uses augmented-system refinement with extended decimal residual calculations; each type is checked against its explicit NIST acceptance bars.
- **Zero-allocation Levenberg-Marquardt.** `LevenbergMarquardtSolver<T>` with optional `LevenbergMarquardtBuffers<T>` pool. The pooled overload produces bit-identical results to the pool-free overload and allocates exactly zero bytes across 1000 warmup-stabilised inner iterations.
- **92 cross-language verification tests** against independent Python references (NIST StRD, `statsmodels`, `scipy`, `scipy.stats`, `scipy.optimize.minimize('SLSQP')`, `numpy`, `scikit-learn`, `arch`, `mpmath`, plus real Fama-French factor data). Every load-bearing public type ships with at least one external cross-check.
- **Zero `Boutquin.*` domain dependencies**, enforced at build time by architecture tests.

### Added

#### Solvers

- **Root finders** (Tier B at `T : IFloatingPointIeee754<T>`): `BrentSolver<T>`, `BisectionSolver<T>`, `NewtonRaphsonSolver<T>`, `MullerSolver<T>`, `SecantSolver<T>`. Generic interfaces `IBracketedRootSolver<T>`, `IUnbracketedRootSolver<T>`. Result record `RootSolverResult<T>`.
- **Levenberg-Marquardt** (Tier B): `LevenbergMarquardtSolver<T>` with Marquardt scaled-diagonal damping, Nielsen 1999 gain-ratio update, central finite-difference Jacobian, optional analytic Jacobian, optional box bounds via projection, relative function tolerance per MINPACK lmder. `LevenbergMarquardtBuffers<T>` caller-owned scratch pool for zero-allocation iteration in hot paths. Result record `MultivariateSolverResult<T>` reporting parameters, half-SSE cost, residuals, parameter covariance, termination reason, and a `BoundsActive` flag. Interface `IMultivariateLeastSquaresSolver<T>`.
- **Ordinary least squares** (Tier A + √): `OrdinaryLeastSquares<T>.Fit(x, y, includeIntercept)` backed by Householder QR with column pivoting. At `T = double`, mixed-precision iterative refinement uses `decimal` residuals for stability on ill-conditioned problems; at `T = decimal`, the full factorisation runs in 28-digit precision. Result record `OlsResult<T>` reporting coefficients, standard errors, residuals, residual sum of squares, residual standard deviation, degrees of freedom, R², and the covariance matrix.
- **Portfolio optimization** (Tier A + √): `ActiveSetQpSolver<T>` implements two standard long-only portfolio QPs — `SolveMinVariance(covariance, minWeight, maxWeight)` (minimises `w′Σw` subject to `1′w = 1` and `lb ≤ w ≤ ub`) and `SolveMeanVariance(covariance, means, riskAversion, minWeight, maxWeight)` (maximises `w′μ − (λ/2)·w′Σw` under the same constraints). Cholesky-based active-set method delegating to `CholeskyDecomposition<T>`; the inner solve uses the identity `Σ_sub⁻¹ · 1` together with the cross-covariance term `Σ_sub⁻¹ · (Σ_cross^T · w_fixed)` so that fixed-bound variables' linear contributions are correctly applied when the problem is reduced to the free block. Uses execution caps of 2N iterations (MinVar) or 3N + 3 iterations (MeanVar), throwing InvalidOperationException on exhaustion; these are not convergence guarantees; `riskAversion = 0` reduces MeanVar to a pure linear programme. `ActiveSetQpSolver` decimal facade preserves pre-generic signatures.

#### Distributions

- **Normal distribution** (Tier C): `NormalDistribution<T>` with `Pdf`, `Cdf`, `InverseCdf`. `CumulativeNormal<T>` uses the Laikov (2025) exponential-free `erf` approximation. `InverseNormal<T>` uses Acklam (2000) polished with two Newton steps against the Laikov CDF.

#### Interpolation

- `IInterpolator<T>` stateless interface. `LinearInterpolator<T>`, `TwoPointLinearInterpolator<T>` (Tier A). `LogLinearInterpolator<T>`, `FlatForwardInterpolator<T>` (Tier B — IEEE only). `MonotoneCubicInterpolator<T>` with arXiv:2402.01324 endpoint improvements, `MonotoneCubicSpline<T>` for O(log n) repeated evaluation, `CubicSplineInterpolator<T>` with five `SplineBoundary<T>` modes (Natural, Clamped, NotAKnot, ModifiedNotAKnot per Jarre 2025, QSpline) — all Tier A + √. `InterpolatorFactory<T>` and `InterpolatorKind` for runtime selection.
- **`MonotoneConvexInterpolator<T>`** (Tier A) — Hagan-West (2006) monotone-convex interpolation (Applied Mathematical Finance 13(2), pp. 89–129) for discount-factor curves. Guarantees **non-negative instantaneous forward rates** across the full maturity range when applied to NCR (normalized cumulative return = `−ln(DF)`) node arrays. The no-arbitrage constraint for yield curves, distinct from `MonotoneCubicInterpolator` which preserves monotonicity of the y-values themselves. `InterpolatorKind.MonotoneConvex = 5` added to the factory.

#### Linear algebra

- All Tier A + √: `CholeskyDecomposition<T>` with unpivoted `Decompose(A)` and pivoted `DecomposePivoted(A, tolerance, maxRank)` for PSD matrices and low-rank approximations (arXiv:2507.20678); `Solve(L, b)` triangular back-substitution. `GaussianElimination<T>` with GERCP randomized partial pivoting (arXiv:2505.02023), deterministic under seed. `JacobiEigenDecomposition<T>` with cyclic Givens rotations, eigenvalues descending with eigenvectors as columns (`EigenResult<T>`). `NearestPsdProjection<T>` with `EigenClip` (one-shot, Higham 1988 closest-in-Frobenius), `Higham` (alternating projection for the nearest correlation matrix), and `IsPsd` check.
- **Principal component analysis** (Tier A + √): `PrincipalComponentAnalysis<T>` built on `JacobiEigenDecomposition<T>`. `Decompose(covarianceMatrix)` for pre-estimated covariance input; `FromReturns(ReturnsMatrix, standardize)` for raw-returns input. `PcaResult<T>` exposes descending eigenvalues, sign-flipped eigenvectors (deterministic across runs), explained-variance ratios, cumulative variance, and mean; `Project(data, k)` and `NumComponentsForExplainedVariance(threshold)` helpers.

#### Statistics

- **Online moments** (Tier A): `WelfordMoments<T>` single-pass mean/variance/stddev with parallel-merge (Chan-Golub-LeVeque 1979). `WelfordMoments<T>.Pearson` online bivariate correlation/covariance.
- **Sample skewness and kurtosis** (Tier A + √): `SampleSkewness<T>.Compute(values)` returns the adjusted Fisher-Pearson standardized third moment with bias-correction factor `n / ((n − 1)(n − 2))`, matching Excel `SKEW` and `scipy.stats.skew(bias=False)`. `SampleExcessKurtosis<T>.Compute(values)` returns the Fisher G₂ excess kurtosis with bias-correction matching Excel `KURT` and `scipy.stats.kurtosis(fisher=True, bias=False)`; uses `long` arithmetic for the integer correction-factor products to prevent `int32` overflow past `n = 1290`. Two-pass algorithm on a `ReadOnlySpan<T>`: Welford mean/variance in pass 1, cubed/fourth standardized deviations in pass 2. `SampleSkewness` and `SampleExcessKurtosis` `decimal`-facade classes preserve pre-generic call sites.
- **Covariance estimators** (Tier A or Tier A + √), all exposing `ICovarianceEstimator<T>.Estimate(ReturnsMatrix<T>)`: `SampleCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `ExponentiallyWeightedCovarianceEstimator<T>`, three Ledoit-Wolf variants (`LedoitWolfShrinkageEstimator<T>`, `LedoitWolfConstantCorrelationEstimator<T>`, `LedoitWolfSingleFactorEstimator<T>`), `OracleApproximatingShrinkageEstimator<T>` (OAS), `QuadraticInverseShrinkageEstimator<T>` (QIS), `DenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>` (Marchenko-Pastur), `DetonedCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `TracyWidomDenoisedCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `DoublySparseEstimator<T>`, `NercomeCovarianceEstimator<T>`, `PoetCovarianceEstimator<T>`.
- **Correlation primitives**: `PearsonCorrelation<T>` (two-pass, Rolling window variant), `RankCorrelation<T>.Spearman`, `RankCorrelation<T>.KendallTauB` (Tier A). `DistanceCorrelation<T>` (Székely-Rizzo-Bakirov 2007), `FisherZTransform<T>` (Tier B).
- **Returns matrix**: `ReturnsMatrix<T>` value type accepting either T×N (`T[,]`) or asset-major (`T[][]`) layouts with explicit construction from either layout; only the legacy non-generic `ReturnsMatrix` facade has implicit conversions. The default method on `ICovarianceEstimator<T>` resolves the layout at the boundary.
- **Backtest statistics** (Tier A with transcendental tail, or Tier B): `DeflatedSharpeRatio<T>` with `DsrResult<T>`. `HaircutSharpe<T>` with Bonferroni/Holm/BHY corrections and `HaircutSharpeResult<T>`. `MinimumTrackRecordLength<T>`. `ProbabilityOfBacktestOverfitting<T>` via CSCV with `PboResult<T>`. `NeweyWestVariance<T>` HAC variance with Bartlett kernel and automatic lag selection. `GtInspiredScore<T>` (Sheppert 2026) four-component composite with `GtInspiredScoreResult<T>`.

#### Monte Carlo

- **Bootstrap resamplers**: `BootstrapResampler<T>` (circular block), `MovingBlockBootstrapResampler<T>` (Künsch / Liu-Singh), `StationaryBootstrapResampler<T>` (Politis-Romano geometric blocks), `WildBootstrapResampler<T>` with `WildBootstrapWeights` (Mammen / Rademacher / Gaussian), `Subsampler.Run<T>` (Politis-Romano-Wolf). `FastDoubleBootstrap` (decimal-only arithmetic) with `PValueTail` parameter for right-tail / left-tail / two-sided conventions. `PolitisWhiteBlockLength.Estimate<T>` for data-driven optimal block length.
- **Monte Carlo engine**: `BootstrapMonteCarloEngine<T>` (Tier A + √) with single-statistic `Run` and multi-statistic `RunMulti` overload (computes K statistics in one pass). Results: `BootstrapMonteCarloResult<T>`, `MultiStatisticBootstrapResult<T>`.
- **Percentile**: `Percentile.Compute<T>` (NumPy `linear` / R `type=7`), `HarrellDavisPercentile.Compute<T>` with embedded incomplete-beta evaluation, `PSquareEstimator<T>` streaming five-marker estimator (Jain-Chlamtac 1985).
- **Quasi-Monte Carlo**: `HaltonSequence<T>` (up to 50 dimensions), `SobolSequence<T>` (Joe-Kuo 2008, up to 32 dimensions). Both Tier C — direction numbers and scrambling constants calibrated for `double`.

#### Random

- `IRandomSource<T>` with `NextULong`, `NextUInt`, `NextDouble`, `NextInt(upperExclusive)` (Lemire arXiv:1805.10941 nearly-divisionless rejection sampling). `Pcg64RandomSource<T>` (PCG-XSL-RR 128/64, stream-independent via `streamId`). `Xoshiro256StarStarRandomSource<T>` (Blackman-Vigna 2018, `Jump()` advances by 2^128). `GaussianSampler<T>` Marsaglia polar method with cached pair. All Tier C — rejection sampling and bit-tricks require IEEE types.

#### Collections

- **`RollingWindow<T>`** — fixed-capacity circular buffer implementing `IEnumerable<T>`. `new(int capacity)`, `Capacity` / `Count` / `IsFull`, indexer, `Add(item)` (overwrites the oldest element when full), `ToArray()`, `Clear()`, and enumerator. Unconstrained `T` — appropriate for any element type, including non-floating-point payloads. Zero domain dependencies: input validation uses BCL `ArgumentOutOfRangeException.ThrowIfNegativeOrZero` rather than a domain guard helper. Lives in `Boutquin.Numerics.Collections`.

#### Internal helpers

- `NumericPrecision<T>` — generic `Sqrt` dispatcher for `T : IFloatingPoint<T>` via compile-time `typeof(T)` branching (dotnet/runtime#74055 precedent). Delegates to `Math.Sqrt` / `MathF.Sqrt` / `Half.Sqrt` / Newton-Raphson for `decimal`.
- `CovarianceHelpers<T>` shared validation and reconstruction primitives used across every estimator.
- `InterpolationHelper.FindInterval<T>` bracket-and-validate logic shared across interpolators.

#### Validation

- **NIST Statistical Reference Datasets** — full coverage across three suites: 9 univariate-summary problems for `WelfordMoments` at 14-digit precision; all 27 nonlinear-regression problems for `LevenbergMarquardtSolver` converging from NIST Start 2 with finite-difference Jacobian; 11 linear-regression problems for `OrdinaryLeastSquares` at `T = double` (coefficient acceptance bars of 1e-12 on ten problems and 1e-7 on Filip; these test thresholds are distinct from NIST's certification precision) and the same 11 at `T = decimal` (1e-13 on ten problems, 1e-6 on Filip).
- **Python cross-checks** — 92 tests under `Boutquin.Numerics.Tests.Verification` cross-check against independent references. Covariance estimators vs paper-faithful numpy ports across three regimes (small well-conditioned, moderate, overconcentrated). OLS vs `statsmodels.api.OLS` at 1e-10 relative. Levenberg-Marquardt vs `scipy.optimize.least_squares(method='lm')` at 1e-5 relative. `SampleSkewness` and `SampleExcessKurtosis` vs `scipy.stats.skew(bias=False)` and `scipy.stats.kurtosis(fisher=True, bias=False)` at 1e-10 absolute across 6 synthetic cases (normal, gamma, negated-gamma, uniform, t(3), small-n). `ActiveSetQpSolver.SolveMinVariance` / `SolveMeanVariance` vs `scipy.optimize.minimize(method='SLSQP')` at 1e-6 per-weight across 5 portfolio cases (including a boxed 3-asset MinVar case that exercises the cross-covariance sub-problem path). Bootstrap resamplers vs `arch.bootstrap.StationaryBootstrap` / `MovingBlockBootstrap` / `optimal_block_length`. Muller's reference validated against `mpmath.findroot(..., solver='muller')` — independent implementation. `NeweyWestVariance` vs `statsmodels.api.OLS(y, ones).fit().get_robustcov_results('HAC', ...)` at 2.4e-15 relative. `MonotoneConvexInterpolator` vs a paper-faithful hand-port of Hagan-West §2.3–§2.6 at 1e-10 absolute (no scipy equivalent exists for the algorithm). `PrincipalComponentAnalysis` with `standardize: true` verified on 60 months of real Fama-French 5-factor returns.
